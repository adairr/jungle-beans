Jungle Beans

Live the Bean!

A trek like no other

Three days out from São Paulo, trekking along the Araguaia River, you stumble upon a plant you've never seen before and not one of the experts can identify. The locals call it the feathered serpent bean and invite you to try it that evening. You learn it's a hardy plant that thrives in multiple climates and has some amazing characteristics. You take some seeds back to the lab in Denver, cultivate the plant, and run additional testing as part of your studies in field Ethnobotany.

Two years later, after unequivocally proving that this mysterious “Jungle Bean” holds an unfathomable amount of medicinal qualities, some truly amazing results emerge. Early clinical trials show it eliminates 95% of the most aggressive forms of cancer and reduces viral load across some of the world's fastest-emerging diseases, not to mention the blissful sense of euphoria felt when ingesting this super plant.

The Bean smear campaign

Almost overnight, some of the largest global pharmaceutical companies lobby to ban the sale and trade of Jungle Beans. Every country in both hemispheres slaps a severe lockdown on this vegetation of the gods, blocking the chance to improve the human condition.

Your Role

You decide that this is unacceptable. You make a pact to build your own distribution network, one airport, one sale at a time. Your colleagues, friends, and family beg you to reconsider the risk you're taking. But you're not going to let corrupt governments, customs agents, mobsters, and questionable street food vendors camped outside of airports stop you from bringing Jungle Beans to the people of Earth!

The briefing

  1. Ground zero is São Paulo, Brazil. You start with $5,000 in your wallet. Buy as much bean product as you can, but leave a little money aside for future flights and travel mishaps. Your goal is to sell Jungle Bean products at all 10 airports and still have money left to retire in style at the end of your journey.
  2. Travel to airports with higher prices, but mind your international network of JB friends who have flagged the “heat” level of each airport — these airports are looking to shut down Jungle Bean distribution, so work quickly and move on. Gaining experience during your journey will lead to leveling up and unlocking the ability to sell more types of Jungle Bean products.
  3. Keep an eye on your health, as the perils of global vegetation distribution are not for the faint of heart. (Hint: if you sell more than 25 units of bean beverage when you touch down at an airport, your contacts will sometimes share a round on the house, which can restore health.)
